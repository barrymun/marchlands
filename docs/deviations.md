# Deviations from the original server

The specs in `docs/spec/` describe what the C++ Spitfire server actually did, bugs included. This file lists where Marchlands does something different, and why. Every tunable value lives in `shared/core/src/balance.ts`.

## Ported exactly

- **Static data.** Building, research and troop costs, times and prerequisites, and items are converted from `config_*` (`tools/legacy-import`).
- **Economy.** Fields (production `100F`/h, storage `10000F` — *(design change)* every city's storage is ×100 (`BALANCE.storageMultiplier`), workers `10F`), cottages, base +100/h, tax income (*(design change)*, see below), hero salaries (`level×20`/h; *(design change)* paid per real hour, unaffected by `GAME_SPEED` (`BALANCE.salaryPerRealHour`); `BALANCE.heroSalaries = false` switches them off), 6-minute population drift (*(design change)* lords' cities have no loyalty, see below).
- **Timing.**
  - Build time: `time × 0.995^management × 0.9^Construction` (demolition is instant, see below).
  - Research time: `time × 0.995^stratagem`.
  - Training time: `× 0.995^power × 0.9^(Military Science | Metal Casting)`.
  - Fortification time: `× 0.995^management × 0.9^Construction`.
- **Prestige** (`GetPrestigeOfAction`) and the refund ratios: demolish 1/3, cancelled research 1/3, cancelled batch 1/3 in progress or 1/1 queued.
- **Heroes.**
  - Stat rolls (`RandomStat`), tavern heroes, hire cost `level×1000`, reward `level×100` for +5 loyalty.
  - Level-up experience `level²×100`, the experience books and the loyalty medals.
  - Hero capture rule: d100 > loyalty, needs a free Feasting Hall slot.
- **March time** (`CalcTroopSpeed`): the slowest unit decides; Compass +10% per level on foot, Horseback Riding +5% per level on mounted and siege, both capped by the Academy level.
- **Battle simulator.** A float32-exact port of `EvonyAge1BattleCalc`, verified against all 36,567 regression cases (`npm test`).
- **NPC cities.** The level 1–10 table, layout and regeneration (`max/80` resources every 6 min). Valley garrisons follow `ValleyData::Reset`. *(design change, 2026-11-01)* Troops and fortifications come back by the second rather than in 6-minute steps: a barbarian city's at `max/10` every 6 game minutes as before but a little each second (`refillCounts`, `regenerateNpc`, `refillAt` in the tile's state), and a free valley's whole again 6 game minutes after a battle, also by the second, a new garrison rolled only once the old one is whole and has stood 6 minutes (from a seed in the tile's state, `valleyDefenders`), where the original rolled a whole new one 6 minutes after the last. Resources and loyalty keep their steps. A gap between waves always lets some troops back, which is what makes landing waves together pay (docs/features/historical-cities.md). Every arrival is fought as of its landing time, so a busy worker doesn't give a garrison extra seconds.
- **Other.** Comfort (blessing and population raising: costs, effects, 15-minute cooldown); the 4×4 state grid.

## Gaps filled

The original either stubbed these or never answered them.

- **Attacks.** Battles against player cities, barbarian cities and valleys:
  - plunder up to the army's load; resources protected by the Warehouse × store percent are safe;
  - barbarian city loyalty −10 to −15 per win; capture at 0 if the attacker's title allows another city, and the city keeps its buildings (*(design change)* lords' cities have no loyalty and cannot be captured);
  - valley occupation (one valley per Town Hall level);
  - honor and hero experience from kills; injured troops through Medicine;
  - hero capture when the attacking army is wiped out;
  - battle reports for every party involved.
- **Other missions.** Transport (to own or allied cities); reinforcement (merge into your own city, or station with an ally or on your own valley); scouting with intel (more detail with Informatics); founding a new city (needs a hero and `BALANCE.newCityMinResource`, 10,000, of each resource).
- **Recalling armies**, and sending home allied armies stationed with you.
- *(design change)* **The Relief Station both ways** (2026-11-03, the maintainer): a march to one of the player's own places (their city or valley) is the sending city's Relief Station multiplier times faster (the original client's `transportStationParam`), and so is the march home from one, so a transport or reinforcement between the player's cities comes back as fast as it went; it was 6 times slower at Relief Station 10 (`reliefMarchMs`, `homeMarchMs`). From anywhere else an army marches home at its own pace.
- *(design change)* **Historic cities** (Age 2, not Age 1; design: docs/features/historical-cities.md, reference: docs/reference/historic-cities.md): 1,938 NPC cities of levels 12 to 18 at Age 2's places (2,081 until 2026-11-02, when migration 0042 left out the level 12s within 9 tiles of a bigger one and Romagna's last two level 14s, as the live map had them: 1, 16, 126 and 1,795) (tile type 13, `core/src/historic.ts`, migration 0031 and `ensureWorld`), with Age 2's garrisons changing by the day, every research at 10 but Archery, loyalty 100 less 3 a win, refilling by the second (`BALANCE.historic`). The win that leaves one at 0 loyalty captures it when the attacker has a free city slot and holds fewer of its level than `holdLimit` (3, 2, 1, 1): it becomes their city (`cities.historic_level`, migration 0032) with every building at level 10 but its fields at its own level, producing in proportion past 10 (`fieldFactorAt`: 55 × level / 10), under a 24-hour truce of its own (`cities.truce_until`) that its holder's beginner protection, truce or holiday never stand in for; its Rally Spot sends a quarter more an army a level step above 10, a War Ensign adding its quarter on top (`maxArmyTroops`); (2026-11-02) it holds one valley per its own level, 12 to 18, past its Town Hall's 10 (`maxValleysFor`). It can't be teleported; (2026-11-03) its fields can be demolished and built back up to its own level, never past it, each level past 10 costing and taking twice the one before (`buildingMaxLevel`, `BALANCE.historic.fieldLevelStep`); and a restarted or deleted player's seat stands again as a whole historic city. A held seat has a loyalty of its own (`cities.loyalty`, migration 0033), back a point every 6 real minutes: once its truce is over each rival's win takes 3 off it, and the win that leaves it at 0 hands it to the winner (within their city slots and limits; `takeSeat`), whole but for its troops, under a new truce; (2026-11-05) all its old holder had in it is lost, their heroes, armies wherever they are, plan waves, market orders and shipments, as nothing moves to another city; its valleys go free and other cities' troops in it go home. Other players' cities still can't be captured. A held seat drafts 4 times a UTC day (`BALANCE.historic.draftsPerDay`) from barbarian cities nobody holds in its area (9 tiles every way from a level 12, 33 from a 14, its state from a 16, the whole map from Atlantis; `inSeatArea`), each draft taking 20% of each of the target's troop types into the seat (`draftTake`; until 2026-11-03 of its largest type only), which the target refills as usual: by itself by default, one draft at the start of each quarter of the day (`draftJob`, the worker, every minute; `cities.draft_next_at`, migration 0038), from the next quarter after the seat was captured or switched to drafting by itself, never making up the quarters before, aimed at the highest-level barbarian cities in its area and, when the seat has a scout, the one of them with the most troops (otherwise at random); or by hand from a barbarian city's tile card (`draftNow`; `setDraftAuto` turns drafting by itself off, migration 0034). (2026-11-02) A seat also drafts from historic cities nobody holds of a lower level in its area (a level 14 from level 12s, and so on; `canDraftFrom`), and its 4 drafts a day each take from a different city (`cities.drafted_tiles`, migration 0041), where Age 2 drafted an NPC city several times a day. Players' cities are never drafted. Each seat pays its holder a tribute in gold every real hour into the seat (`BALANCE.historic.tribute`: 2,000 at 12 up to 60,000 at Atlantis), the holder of a state's capital gets 20% and Atlantis's 10% of the market fees buyers pay (from that state, from anywhere; `payFeeShares`, as gold shipments), and the two give titles: Ruler of the state, Sovereign of Atlantis (`seatTitle`). While a player holds a state's capital, the state's barbarian cities answer to no faction: they leave the factions' trade, attacking, plundering or drafting them changes no standing, and they pay the capital 5 gold a real hour per member level (`stateTribute`, `cities.state_tribute`, migration 0035). (2026-11-05) Holding one costs nothing more than any city, and it stays held whatever it lacks: the upkeep and the unrest that made a neglected seat rise up, both added 2026-11-01, are gone. A quarter of its tributes go to the holder's alliance, paid to the other members every real hour (`BALANCE.historic.allianceShare`, `engine/seat-share.ts`).
- *(design change)* **Camp times** (design: docs/features/camp-times.md; Age 2's "camp time", docs/reference/historic-cities.md, Timing): an army can be set to land at a chosen second, up to 24 hours ahead (`arriveAt` on the send body, `BALANCE.landAtMaxAheadMs`). It waits at home (its troops, hero and Rally Spot slot taken at once) and leaves in time to land at the start of that second; its target sees nothing until it leaves (a timed `army.depart` event), and recalling it before then brings it back within a second. Armies due at the same moment land in the order they were sent (the worker takes timed events by `due_at`, then `id`). The client enters and shows these times in UTC by the server's clock (the push socket's server time, `lib/server-clock.ts`). Wave plans (`services/plans.ts`) land each of their waves its place in the plan's order in milliseconds into the plan's second, so a plan's waves keep its order whoever sends first.

- **Market.**
  - Escrow when an order is placed; price-time priority matching at the resting order's price; a 0.5% fee paid by the buyer.
  - *(design change)* **Instant settlement**: the lord placing an order is paid in the same transaction; lords whose resting orders are hit are paid within a moment (zero-time shipments). `BALANCE.marketShipmentBaseMs > 0` restores Evony's travel time (60 min ÷ Marketplace level). *(design change)* The **Merchant Fleet** item (halved that travel time) is removed from the game: not sold, not an Amulet prize (`REMOVED_ITEMS` in core); migration 0007 took it out of lords' items.
  - *(design change)* **Computer merchant** (`core/src/merchant.ts`, `BALANCE.merchant`): always buys and sells every resource in any amount, a market of last resort. It sells at its reference price × (1 + spread/2) and buys at × (1 − spread/2); lords' orders at a better or equal price are filled first, so an order inside the spread beats it. **Currently fixed** (`BALANCE.merchant.fixedPrice = 1`, `spread = 0.3`): the reference is 1.00 for every resource and never moves, so the merchant buys at 0.85 and sells at 1.15. Until 2026-10-22 it traded at exactly 1.00 with no spread, so no lord could beat it and every trade went through it. With `fixedPrice: null` the reference instead moves with the merchant's trades along an exponential curve (fills are priced along the curve, so splitting orders or round-tripping gains nothing) and drifts back to its base with a 6-game-hour half-life; a worker job lets the merchant fill resting orders its drifting price has crossed. The curve's `depth` (1,000,000) is far too shallow for today's trades (100–350 million a trade): it would need retuning before use.
  - **Market and limit orders**, a quote endpoint, and a trade log (`market_trades`) with recent trades. *(design change)* **Price candles** of a minute, an hour and a day of real time (`market_candles`, unaffected by `GAME_SPEED`), updated with every trade; the worker deletes minute candles after 7 days and hour candles after 90 (`BALANCE.marketCandles`). Migration 0027 built them from the trades made before.
  - Order cap of 2 resting orders per Marketplace level; cancellation refunds.
  - *(design change)* **NPC factions trade** (`core/src/npc-economy.ts`, `BALANCE.npcTrade`; design: docs/features/market-and-factions.md). Barbarian cities from level 5 belong to one of five factions by zone (`core/src/factions.ts`) and trade in groups, one per zone and level. Each game hour a group makes and uses 2% of its target (its members' full stock), more making of its faction's strengths and its zone's land (its valleys against the average zone), more use of its faction's shortages, the world balanced overall, a seeded random wander on each faction's making, and a drift back to target (half-life a game day). Every real minute one auction per resource sets the world price, (world target ÷ world stock)^e inside the merchant's band (at its ask when the world is 25% short), and groups trade towards what they want at it. Each group holds gold: its treasury is its members' gold in the NPC table, made and used at 2% a game hour (the Italian League making 50% more) and drifting back like its stock; it buys in the auction only what its gold pays for. Lords trade with the factions' shared stock along that curve, 1% above and below it, between other lords' orders and the merchant, and are paid only while the factions hold gold (the merchant buys the rest); the factions also fill resting orders their price crosses each tick. Plundering a member lowers its group's stock. The factions' auctions go into the price candles (`market_candles`) and a ticker of made-up single trades; lords' trades with them are `market_trades` rows marked `npc` (migration 0028).
  - *(design change)* **Standing with the factions** (`core/src/reputation.ts`, `BALANCE.reputation`; design: docs/features/market-and-factions.md, Reputation's rules). Each lord has a standing with each faction from -100 to 100 (table `faction_standing`, migration 0030), drifting back to 0 with a half-life of 3 game days. A city trades with the factions through the faction holding its zone, at a spread its lord's standing with it sets: 2% (1% each way) at 0, none at 100, 10% just above -50; at -50 or below that faction won't trade with the city (the merchant and other lords still do). Any attack on a faction member (an unheld barbarian city from level 5) costs 0.3 standing a level with its faction, a capture 20 more; a won attack on a bandit camp (levels 1-4) gains 0.1 a level with the faction whose land it's on; trading with the factions gains 1 a 20M gold with the city's home faction, at most 5 a game day. Battle reports against barbarian cities carry the change. Historic cities (2026-11-01, `seatStanding`, `draftStanding`, `BALANCE.reputation.seat`): any attack on an unheld one costs 0.3 a level with the faction whose state it stands in (Atlantis counts as the Saxon League's), capturing it 20 more (40 for a state's capital or Atlantis), and on a capture each faction bordering that one (`factionRivals`) gives the captor half the capture's cost; each draft costs 1 with the drafted city's faction (nothing from a bandit camp). Battle and draft reports carry the changes.
  - *(design change)* **Order limit**: a lord places and cancels at most 10 orders a minute (a burst of 10, then one more every 6 s of real time, `BALANCE.marketOrderLimit`), counted in the API's memory; past it the API answers 429 `market.slowDown` with Retry-After. Quotes are free. So a bot outside the game can't flood the book or out-trade lords by speed.
